A SIMPLE 2-3 MINUTE DEMO
1. Keep Campus crossing selected. Click Compare all.
   Say: "All four algorithms search the same map. BFS finds fewest moves. A* minimizes travel cost, including crowded cells and uncertainty."
   Here BFS costs 40; A* costs 20.6. Both take 20 moves.
2. Select Crowded laboratory. Click Compare all.
   Say: "The cheaper path can be longer: A* takes 26 moves at cost 31, while BFS takes 20 moves at cost 50."
3. Select Campus crossing again. Enable simulated sensor evidence and Sensor reports an obstacle.
   Say: "Bayes' rule updates the blockage probability from 15% to 60%. A decision rule closes the purple zone at 50%."
   Click Find route. Turn the warning off to show the posterior falling to 2.9%.
4. Click Reset. Select A*. Click Run mission.
   While the robot travels, click Block next route cell.
   Say: "A new obstacle invalidates the remaining route. The robot replans from its current position, delivers the parcel and returns to the depot."
5. Optional: choose Disconnected campus, then Find route.
   Say: "The agent reports that no route exists instead of pretending delivery succeeded."

SHORT EXPLANATION
CampusFlow is a classical AI simulation of a delivery robot. Its state space is a campus grid. Four search algorithms choose routes, Bayes' rule updates a simulated sensor belief, and a finite-state controller manages loading, delivery and return. A new obstacle causes the agent to search again.

COMMON VIVA QUESTIONS
Why is this AI without a neural network?
Classical AI includes intelligent agents, state-space search, heuristic reasoning, probability and planning. These are directly in this course syllabus.

What does A* calculate?
f(n) = g(n) + h(n): cost so far plus estimated remaining cost. h is Manhattan distance.

Why is the heuristic valid?
Only horizontal/vertical moves are allowed and each move costs at least 1. Manhattan distance never overestimates remaining cost.

Does BFS always find the cheapest route?
It finds the fewest moves here, but unequal terrain costs mean that may not be the cheapest route.

Are the sensor readings real?
No. The probabilities and evidence are illustrative simulation inputs. A positive report gives 60%; a negative report gives approximately 2.9%.

Is this a full planning engine or an autonomous physical robot?
It is a software simulation. A finite-state mission controller calls route search for delivery and return; it is not a general STRIPS or Graphplan implementation.
